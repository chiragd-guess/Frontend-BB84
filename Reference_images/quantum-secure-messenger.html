About This Simulation

Quantum Secure Messenger walks through the BB84 protocol, the first quantum key distribution scheme, published by Bennett and Brassard in 1984. It lets Alice and Bob agree on a shared secret key using polarized photons, with the laws of quantum mechanics guaranteeing that any eavesdropper leaves a trace.

Alice prepares a string of photons in random bases and sends them across the quantum channel. Bob measures each one in a randomly chosen basis of his own. Afterward, the two compare bases over an ordinary channel and keep only the results where their choices matched — that surviving string becomes the raw key.

Before trusting the key, they sacrifice a sample of it to estimate the quantum bit error rate (QBER). A clean channel gives a low QBER; an eavesdropper measuring photons in transit disturbs their states and drives the error rate up, exposing the intrusion.

Use the Noise slider and Eve toggle in the menu to see how each factor pushes the QBER, and whether the channel still comes out secured on the other side.
